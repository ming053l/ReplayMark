\begin{abstract}
We study a watermark for masked diffusion language models whose detector reruns the model
instead of inspecting token identities alone. ReTrace compares each observed token's
likelihood under two replayable context ablations; a keyed orientation determines which
response sign counts as a match. Reproducible, orientation-independent carrier selection
gives the match count an exact conditional $\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12)$ null. During
blockwise decoding, bounded rejection sampling favors model proposals with the requested
sign and falls back to an unchecked draw. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the best evaluated
512-token settings obtain $0.70$--$0.80$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR with matched-control GPT-2
perplexity ratios of $0.87\times$--$0.74\times$. The channel is useful but costly: detection
requires 144 masked sequence evaluations, trails strong token-based baselines, and drops
from $0.35$ to $0.05$ after re-denoising $10\%$ of positions. ReTrace establishes a
model-response verification channel while exposing its quality, compute, and robustness
limits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ReTrace shows that keyed, replayable changes in a DLM's predictions can form a watermark
channel with an exact conditional null. Balanced gating identifies usable positions,
resampling embeds sign evidence, and re-masking reconstructs it without a generation log.
The current operating point is a proof of concept: verification is expensive, evidence is
edit-fragile, and evaluation covers one checkpoint. Scaling response replay and anchoring
challenges to content are the principal next steps.
