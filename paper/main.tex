\title{ReTrace: Model-Response Watermarking\\for Diffusion Language Models}
\author{Anonymous}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Existing language-model watermarks are detected from token patterns. We detect ours from
\emph{how a diffusion language model responds when the generated text is partially masked
and reconstructed}. Because such a model is trained to recover masked tokens from arbitrary
surviving context, a finished text can be pushed back through the operator that produced
it: corrupted with a secret key, re-denoised, and its response read. ReTrace places a
watermark in that response. A keyed pair of context ablations induces a per-position
log-likelihood contrast, and the watermark asks the sign of that contrast to agree with a
keyed orientation, giving a test statistic that is a sum of bounded indicators with a
finite-sample exact $\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12)$ null. Embedding never overrides the
model: decoding follows the reference block schedule, and the watermark only decides
whether a sampled proposal is emitted or redrawn. We also report the
negative result that motivated this design --- post-hoc token substitution is priced out on
a well-trained diffusion language model, and, because both are governed by the sharpness of
the same conditionals, \emph{improving generation quality reduces post-hoc
watermarkability}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.72\linewidth]{figures/fig_retrace_rcurve.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Retries buy detection without model forwards.} A retry redraws from the
same conditional row, so $R$ costs arithmetic rather than compute, and the quality pairing
against the matched control measured $0.97\times$--$0.98\times$ at $R{=}1$--$2$ on two
independent prompt sets. Reference lines are the local reproductions of dgMARK and KGW at
their own quality costs.}
\label{fig:rcurve}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The retry curve reaches the baselines' detection range.}
On held-out prompts ($n{=}30$, $512$ tokens, per-document nonces), raising the retry
budget moves the presence rate from $0.617$ to $0.674$ between $R{=}2$ and $R{=}4$, and
$\mathrm{TPR}@1\%$ from $0.73$ to $\mathbf{0.83}$ --- level with locally reproduced dgMARK
($0.86$ at $1.23\times$ perplexity). The matched-control quality pairing for these arms is
printing as this draft is written; at $R{=}1$ and $R{=}2$ it measured $0.97\times$--$0.98\times$
across two independent prompt sets, and the mechanism predicts the cost stays sub-bit per
two-sided carrier independent of $R$.

\paragraph{Detector choice settles on the simplest.}
On identical held-out caches, the gated count, the gated LLR and the all-position LLR are
indistinguishable at $R{=}1$ ($0.38$ at $1\%$ each; ungated counting is strictly worse at
$0.33$), and every variant holds its null on the control arm. The deployed detector is
therefore the gated indicator with its exact binomial null; the likelihood machinery adds
nothing where the per-position evidence is weak, because negative evidence at one-sided
positions only materialises once the embedder has visibly tried and failed --- which takes
retries.

\paragraph{Robustness is the weak axis, and the mechanism says why.}
Under same-model re-denoising of $10\%$ of positions, $R{=}1$ detection falls from $0.35$
to $0.05$ at $1\%$ (deletion: likewise), while the attacked control stays at $\alpha$ ---
the attack does not trigger the detector, it erases the evidence. The fragility is
structural: the challenge context of every block includes all earlier blocks, so an edit
anywhere perturbs the contrast of every later position. Whether the higher rates at
$R{=}4$--$8$ buy usable attack headroom is measured on the saved held-out outputs.

\paragraph{Superseded preliminary numbers (presence-only, $256$ tokens, $n{=}20$).}
With every carrier feeding a single presence test, the unwatermarked null sits at a match
rate of $0.498$ ($n_{\mathrm{sync}}{=}123$ per document), and the watermarked arms reach
$\mathrm{TPR}@1\% = 0.25$ at $R{=}1$ and $0.50$ at $R{=}2$, doubling with the retry budget
exactly as the per-position acceptance analysis predicts. Two things this section still
withholds, deliberately. \emph{Quality:} every perplexity so far was measured against a
differently-scheduled reference generator, and the watermarked arms at $R{=}1$ and $R{=}2$
showing the same $\Delta$NLL is the signature of that confound; a matched-scheduler
control is running and its paired $\Delta$NLL is the number that will be reported.
\emph{Scale:} these are $n{=}20$ documents of which about half clear the evaluable-length
threshold in both arms --- the freeze decision uses $n{\geq}30$ valid documents at $512$
tokens, where the presence count doubles again.

The mechanism study that led here is summarised by three measurements: the per-position
acceptance mass under the live decoder is bimodal (quartiles $0.021/0.601/0.982$), a third
of carriers hold under $0.1$ mass on the side the key requests, and truncating the live
row to top-$p\,0.9$ leaves a median two-sided mass of $0.000$ --- inside the model's
plausible set the reconstruction response is nearly deterministic, so match rate is bought
in the tail of the distribution, which is exactly where text quality is paid.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ReTrace separates the two roles a watermark usually conflates. The decoding order is where
the watermark is \emph{placed}, cheaply, by choosing when a model-preferred token is
committed. The model's reconstruction response is where it is \emph{read back}, from the
finished text alone. Whether that separation converts enough
steerable positions into detection power at a fixed quality budget is the question the
remaining experiments answer.
