\begin{abstract}
We propose Shibboleth, a model-response watermarking method for masked diffusion language models
(DLMs). Unlike an autoregressive sampler, a masked DLM can be re-evaluated on its own output
under an arbitrary remasking of the context. In the evaluated DLMs, remasking the context changes
the likelihood of an emitted token according to the model weights. These changes define a
watermarking channel between the text and the model rather than within token identities. Shibboleth evaluates a keyed family of context probes
against each masked block, admits as carriers only the unresolved positions whose block-masked
conditional retains mass on both response signs, and resamples at those carriers from the live
conditional until the requested sign is committed. Unlike logit-bias methods it
leaves the model's probabilities untouched and conditions only on which model-drawn proposal is
accepted; unlike order-steering methods, it does not alter the decoding schedule. Because the admitting gate is invariant to swapping two probes, it stays blind to the sign the
key will request, and carriers may therefore be discovered adaptively without disturbing the
null.
Detection re-masks the finished text, rebuilds the identical probes and carriers, and counts
keyed sign matches. On text written without the key, the match count is binomial; the resulting
$p$-values are exact at finite sample size, including when carriers are selected adaptively, without
a threshold fitted to a calibration corpus. At $1\%$ FPR, Shibboleth reaches $0.80$ TPR on
LLaDA-8B-Instruct and $0.90$ on Dream-7B-Instruct. On LLaDA, the token-based detectors reach
$0.90$--$0.93$ TPR; Shibboleth's $0.74\times$ perplexity ratio is the only ratio below its matched
unwatermarked baseline in that comparison. On Dream, Shibboleth has the highest measured TPR
under settings transferred unchanged from LLaDA. Verification requires 144 masked evaluations
per 512-token document. Under random $10\%$ re-denoising, TPR falls from $0.35$ to $0.05$ at
$R=1$ and from $0.88$ to $0.50$ at $R=16$; contiguous edits preserve carriers preceding the edit.
The method therefore assumes verifier access to the evaluated checkpoint and is not designed for
model-free screening.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Shibboleth computes a provenance statistic from a masked DLM's responses to keyed probes of its
own output rather than from a keyed pattern in token identities. Because carrier selection is
completed before the keyed orientation is consulted, adaptive carrier discovery preserves the
binomial null. The method also preserves the reference decoding schedule, but verification
requires the specified checkpoint; a key--token hash does not have this requirement. Under
settings transferred unchanged from LLaDA to Dream, the two token-local baselines have lower
detection rates, and dgMARK reduces Dream GSM8K accuracy by $0.28$, whereas Shibboleth's probe
is recomputed with the Dream checkpoint. Token-local methods retain lower verification cost and
do not require checkpoint-specific replay.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
That guarantee is nevertheless one-sided, since soundness holds for any text written without the
key while nothing is claimed in the other direction, and editing degrades detection: a single
same-model denoising pass over a tenth of the positions takes TPR from $0.35$ to $0.05$ at
$R=1$, and at $R=16,\kappa=0.05$ it reduces detection from $0.88$ to $0.50$ at $1\%$ FPR
($n=16$); contiguous edits leave the measured TPR unchanged even at $30\%$ of positions, since
damage propagates forward only. The measured edit results are limited to random and contiguous
re-denoising under the reported redundancy settings. Verification requires 144 masked evaluations
per document, compared with zero for the token-local detectors in Table~\ref{tab:baselines}.
The response statistic is bound to one checkpoint as well; different weights or numerical precision
fall outside the stated replay assumptions. Finally, the evaluation covers two masked diffusion
models, one prompt corpus, cohorts of twenty to fifty documents, perplexity ratios read
against each method's own unwatermarked baseline plus a single 50-problem GSM8K probe, and
provenance detection only, with the
payload construction of Section~\ref{sec:theory} never measured.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Content-anchored probes can be evaluated as a method for limiting desynchronisation after
insertions or deletions, and copy--paste attacks, where a per-block minimum statistic may differ
from pooled counting. Sequential testing can reduce verification work because the binomial tail
is monotone in the accumulated match count. Quantised contrasts may reduce sensitivity to
checkpoint precision at the cost of carrier capacity. Query-adaptive attacks also require separate
evaluation because detector responses reveal key-dependent information. A combined system could
use a token-local first stage followed by model-response verification on selected documents.
