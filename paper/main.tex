\begin{abstract}
We propose Shibboleth, a model-response watermarking method for masked diffusion language models
(DLMs). Unlike an autoregressive sampler, a masked DLM can be re-evaluated on its own output
under an arbitrary remasking of the context. Because a practical DLM, unlike an ideal predictor, shifts the likelihood
of an emitted token by an amount its own weights determine, a watermarking channel opens in the
relation between a text and the model rather than in the tokens. Shibboleth evaluates a keyed family of context probes
against each masked block, admits as carriers only the unresolved positions whose block-masked
conditional retains mass on both response signs, and resamples at those carriers from the live
conditional until the requested sign is committed. Unlike logit-bias methods it
leaves the model's probabilities untouched and conditions only on which model-drawn proposal is
accepted, and unlike order-steering methods it spends none of the decoding schedule. Because the admitting gate is invariant to swapping two probes, it stays blind to the sign the
key will request, and carriers may therefore be discovered adaptively without disturbing the
null.
Detection re-masks the finished text, rebuilds the identical probes and carriers, and counts
keyed sign matches, whose distribution on text written without the key is exactly binomial, and
$p$-values are therefore exact at finite sample size and under carriers the method itself chose,
which no threshold fitted to a corpus can promise. It
reaches $0.80$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR on LLaDA-8B-Instruct — below the strongest token-based
detector there, at the only perplexity ratio in that comparison reading below its own
unwatermarked baseline — and $0.90$ on Dream-7B-Instruct, where every token-local baseline
weakens under transfer and the model-response channel is the strongest detector measured. Verification in exchange costs one rerun of the
checkpoint, and evidence written against a prefix degrades under editing — collapsing at low
redundancy, halving at high — leaving the method
suited to a verifier that holds the checkpoint rather than to open screening.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Shibboleth argues provenance from a keyed probe that a masked DLM answers about its own output
rather than from a pattern left in the tokens, and because the gate is settled before the key
names the sign it accepts, carriers may be discovered adaptively at no cost to the detector's
exact $p$-values. That trade is worth making where the decoding
schedule must not change and the evidence wanted is of the kind a specified model can vouch for,
which a key--token hash cannot supply, since anyone holding the key can compute it without the
generator. Absent either constraint, a token-local watermark is cheaper and easier to
resynchronise after edits.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
That guarantee is nevertheless one-sided, since soundness holds for any text written without the
key while nothing is claimed in the other direction, and editing degrades detection: a single
same-model denoising pass over a tenth of the positions takes TPR from $0.35$ to $0.05$ at
$R=1$, and even at the strongest setting it halves detection ($0.88$ to $0.50$ at $1\%$ FPR,
$n=16$), so Shibboleth claims resilience to light editing at high redundancy and nothing
beyond it. Cost is the second boundary, since 144 masked evaluations per document rule out
screening a corpus. The evidence is bound to one checkpoint as well, and a verifier holding
different weights, or the same weights at a different precision, consequently cannot reproduce
the responses at all. Finally, the evaluation is itself narrow, covering two masked diffusion
models, one prompt corpus, cohorts of twenty to fifty documents, quality proxies read as ratios
against each method's own unwatermarked baseline, and provenance detection only, with the
payload construction of Section~\ref{sec:theory} never measured.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Each boundary suggests its own repair. Anchoring probe patterns to content rather than to
absolute positions would stop an edit from desynchronising every carrier after it, which is the
single change most likely to turn an abrupt collapse into a graceful decline, and the remaining
attack to run is the localized one — contiguous edits and copy--paste — where a per-block
min-statistic should finally beat the pooled count. Verification cost
is the easier target, since the binomial tail is monotone in the matches so far and a sequential
test could stop once the evidence is decisive.
Loosening the checkpoint requirement in turn asks for a contrast quantised coarsely enough that numerical
drift cannot flip a sign, trading a little capacity for portability. Adaptive adversaries deserve
their own study, since a detector answering queries leaks something about its key.
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} finally argues for composition rather than a winner, with a cheap
token-local screen deciding what is worth a model-response check.
