\begin{abstract}
Most language-model watermarks verify a keyed pattern in the emitted token sequence. We
study a different signal for masked diffusion language models: how the model reconstructs
a completed text after keyed parts of its context are hidden. ReTrace compares the
log-likelihood of each observed token under two context ablations. A secret orientation bit
specifies the desired sign of this contrast, and the detector counts sign agreements. With
domain-separated pseudorandom functions, this count has an exact finite-sample
$\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\tfrac12)$ null for any text generated independently of the key.
During blockwise decoding, ReTrace rejection-samples model proposals at reproducible
carrier positions and uses an unconditional fallback after a fixed retry budget. This
changes the sampling distribution and is not claimed to be distortion-free, but it never
inserts a token that the model did not propose. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the best evaluated
512-token settings obtain $0.70$--$0.80$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR with GPT-2 perplexity ratios of
$0.87\times$--$0.74\times$ relative to matched controls; verification requires 144 masked
sequence evaluations. Detection remains below strong token-based baselines and falls from
$0.35$ to $0.05$ after same-model re-denoising of $10\%$ of positions. Finally, a post-hoc
substitution ablation explains the design choice: sharper, better-performing denoising
conditionals leave fewer plausible alternatives and therefore less room for substitution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ReTrace uses a diffusion language model as part of the verifier. It masks a completed text
under two keyed context ablations and tests whether the model's likelihood contrast agrees
with secret orientation bits. Domain separation gives the resulting count an exact
binomial null, while block masking lets the verifier reconstruct the same challenges used
during generation without access to the decoding trajectory.

The experiments establish a trade-off rather than a uniformly better watermark. Guided
resampling yields useful detection at low measured perplexity cost, and a plausibility
floor prevents large retry budgets from selecting very unlikely proposals. In return,
verification requires model inference and the watermark is fragile to post-editing. The
evaluation is also limited to one model and modest sample sizes. These limitations make
ReTrace most appropriate as a proof of concept for model-response verification, not yet as
a replacement for inexpensive token-based detectors. Improving edit robustness and
validating the response channel across model families are the main next steps.
