\subsection{Why substitution cannot pay}
\label{sec:v1}

With the observable and the statistic fixed, the embedder's task sounds elementary:
make the committed tokens answer with the key. The natural first design does it by
\emph{substitution} --- generate normally, then at keyed positions replace the model's
token with an alternative whose contrast points the right way, subject to a per-token
fluency budget $\tau$ (admissible iff $p(w)/p(w^\star) \geq e^{-\tau}$). Its measured
failure dictates the final design.

The first obstacle is that there is usually nothing to substitute. A well-trained
denoiser's conditionals are sharp: across $36$ configurations spanning three
carrier-selection rules, three budgets and two guidance weights, the \emph{median} keyed
position admits exactly one token at $\tau = 3$ ($p(w)/p(w^\star) \geq 0.05$), with no
truncation of the candidate slate. The best configuration inside a $1.35\times$
perplexity budget reaches only $\mathrm{TPR}@1\% = 0.10$, against $0.86$ for locally
reproduced dgMARK at $1.23\times$.

Sharper still, the obstacle grows with model quality. When a confidence-ranking defect
in our sampler was repaired --- draft perplexity moving from $22.2$ to $9.5$ --- the
denoiser's entropy halved ($0.655 \to 0.319$) and the admissible sets shrank by
$34$--$60\,\%$ ($|A(3)|$: $2.5 \to 1.6$; $|A(6)|$: $14.1 \to 5.6$). Substitution buys
evidence with fluency at a rate set by the sharpness of the model's conditionals, and
\important{improving a diffusion language model's generation quality reduces its
post-hoc watermarkability}, because both quantities are governed by the same sharpness.
Nor does moving the substitution to generation time escape this: a guidance table valid
during generation needs every non-carrier token final first, and the two-phase schedule
that forces costs $1.98\times$ perplexity \emph{before any watermark} --- more than
dgMARK's entire cost.

What these measurements leave standing is a design constraint rather than a tuning
problem: an embedder must never override the model's choice --- it may only choose
\emph{among} tokens the model itself proposes. The next subsection builds exactly that
embedder.
