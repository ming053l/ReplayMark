\paragraph{Schedule fidelity.}
The clearest advantage over prefix-based adaptation is that ReTrace does not serialize the
DLM. In our control, the left-to-right schedule needed by KGW raises PPL by
$1.17\times$ relative to block decoding and produces high repetition before any watermark
strength is considered. ReTrace also differs from dgMARK: dgMARK turns order sensitivity
into its channel, whereas ReTrace treats the reference order as an invariant and acts only
after a live probability row exists. This distinction matters when the production decoder
has a validated latency--quality schedule that should not be changed. It is not a free
advantage: constructing the RRB adds $28\%$ generation evaluations.

\paragraph{Model-bound evidence.}
A token-only detector asks whether token identities correlate with a key. RRV asks a
different question: whether the emitted tokens exhibit keyed responses when a specified
model reconstructs them under counterfactual context views. The evidence is therefore
bound jointly to text, context, and checkpoint. This can be useful for provenance settings
where the verifier controls the model artifact and wants evidence tied to its behavior,
rather than to any generator capable of reproducing a token histogram. The same property
is a liability for open verification because a different checkpoint need not reproduce the
responses.

\paragraph{Exactness after adaptive carrier discovery.}
BCG chooses carriers and ablation pairs from the completed prefix and model probabilities;
the number and identity of trials are consequently data dependent. Nevertheless, after
conditioning on those choices, the orientation bits remain independent fair coins and the
binomial tail is exact. The advantage is not merely replacing a $z$-score with a binomial
function: it is preserving finite-sample validity after model-driven carrier discovery.
The measured KGW failure without bigram deduplication illustrates why dependence and trial
definition cannot be treated as implementation details.

\paragraph{Boundary of the claim.}
Table~\ref{tab:design-comparison} makes clear that ReTrace is not uniformly superior.
KGW and dgMARK have zero-inference detectors, stronger local TPR, and token-local evidence
that is easier to synchronize after edits. ReTrace is preferable only when preserving the
native DLM schedule, obtaining a replayable model-response statistic, and retaining exact
conditional calibration are worth an expensive checkpoint-specific verifier. A practical
system could also combine these channels: a cheap token-local watermark for routine
screening and RRV as a higher-cost confirmation layer. This composition is a design
direction, not an evaluated result.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Results cover one checkpoint, one corpus, small samples, and automatic quality proxies.
Cross-model generality, human or downstream quality, scalable verification, adaptive
attacks, and content-anchored synchronization remain open.
