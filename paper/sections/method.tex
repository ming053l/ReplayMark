\subsection{Probing the Model and Selecting Carriers}
\label{sec:observable}

Steps 2--3 split candidates by which masked-prefix probe assigns more probability. A position is
usable only when both groups have sufficient live probability; the key then chooses which group
the emitted token should favour.

Formally, ReplayMark constructs $L$ pseudorandom probe patterns
$D_{b,\ell}\subset F_b$, where each pattern specifies prefix positions to hide. It also masks the
current block $B_b$ and every later position. Let $\lambda_{i,\ell}(v)$ denote the resulting
log-probability of vocabulary item $v$ at position $i$ under pattern $\ell$.
The block and suffix are hidden because neither was available when the probe was created. The
verifier can therefore reconstruct the same state from the completed sequence instead of seeing
later tokens that would alter the probabilities. Probing a block costs $L+1$ full-sequence model
calls: $L$ masked-prefix probes and one evaluation with the full prefix visible.

For a pair $(\ell,\ell')$, define the response
$g_i^{\ell,\ell'}(z)=\lambda_{i,\ell'}(z)-\lambda_{i,\ell}(z)$. Let
$p_i^{\mathrm{base}}$ be the block-masked distribution with no hidden prefix. The aggregate
probability assigned to either response direction, and a symmetric score for the pair, are
\begin{equation}
q_{i,\pm}^{\ell,\ell'}=\sum_z p_i^{\mathrm{base}}(z)
                  \mathbf{1}[\pm g_i^{\ell,\ell'}(z)>0],\qquad
S_i^{\ell,\ell'}=2\min(q_{i,+}^{\ell,\ell'},q_{i,-}^{\ell,\ell'}).
\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
The score is twice the smaller total. It approaches one when the live distribution is divided
evenly between the two response groups and approaches zero when one group has little probability.
At each position, ReplayMark keeps the pair with the largest $S_i$ and selects the position when
$S_i>s_{\min}$, subject to an optional per-block cap. Choosing the best pair raises the measured
mean score from $0.28$ for a fixed near/far pair to $0.45$. Figure~\ref{fig:carriermap} shows where
this criterion retains or skips positions in one saved LLaDA output.
\input{figures/fig_carrier_viz}

Crucially, swapping $\ell$ and $\ell'$ leaves $S_i$ unchanged while reversing every response
sign. Carrier selection therefore cannot favour the direction later requested by the key.

After the carrier is fixed, the key assigns one group to the target bit. Formally, a separate hash
domain supplies $\epsilon_i\in\{-1,+1\}$, while $w_i\in\{-1,+1\}$ represents the provenance or
payload bit. Together they decide which response direction counts as a match:
\begin{equation}
m_i(z)=\mathbf{1}[w_i\epsilon_i g_i(z)>0].
\label{eq:m}
\end{equation}
If $g_i(z)=0$, a second domain-separated bit assigns the outcome. Such a tie cannot be steered,
but it remains an independent fair outcome for detection.

\subsection{Embedding Without Changing the Schedule}
\label{sec:embedding}

The reference decoder still chooses the next position and supplies its live distribution $p_i$.
Unselected positions are sampled once as usual. At a selected carrier, ReplayMark accepts a
candidate only if it has the requested response and is not too far below the most likely token:
\begin{equation}
A_i=\left\{z:
 w_i\epsilon_i g_i(z)>0\ \text{ and }\
 p_i(z)\geq \kappa\max_v p_i(v)
\right\}.
\label{eq:accept-set}
\end{equation}
It draws from the same $p_i$ up to $R$ times and commits the first member of $A_i$. If every
proposal misses, it takes one fresh unchecked draw. All retries reuse $p_i$, so they add no model
call and do not alter the commit order.

Let $a_i=p_i(A_i)$ be the probability of satisfying both conditions, and let
$q_i=\sum_z p_i(z)m_i(z)$ be the chance that an unchecked draw scores as a match. The committed
token comes from $p_i(\cdot\mid A_i)$ with probability $1-(1-a_i)^R$ and from $p_i$ otherwise.
Its match probability is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Pr[m_i=1]=1-(1-a_i)^R(1-q_i).
\label{eq:hit}
\end{equation}
With no plausibility floor and no ties, $a_i=q_i$, giving $1-(1-q_i)^{R+1}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:hit}
separates the two controls: $R$ determines how many chances the sampler has to find an acceptable
token, while $\kappa$ limits how unlikely that token may be relative to the live argmax. Larger $R$
can strengthen detection but changes the sampling distribution; $\kappa$ does not constrain the
unchecked fallback or guarantee semantic quality, which we measure separately.

\subsection{Watermark Detection}
\label{sec:theory}

The verifier re-masks each completed block and suffix, reconstructs the probe pairs and carriers,
and evaluates $m_i(y_i)$. It needs no rejected samples or generation trace. For provenance
detection, the selected carriers form a pool $P$ with $w_i=+1$; a payload implementation would
keep a separate pool for each message bit. The document statistic and its one-sided $p$-value are
\begin{equation}
T=\sum_{i\in P}m_i,\qquad
p_{\mathrm{det}}=\Pr\!\left[\mathrm{Binomial}(|P|,\tfrac12)\geq T\right].
\label{eq:test}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact false-positive control]
\label{prop:fpr}
Assume the hash domains behave as independent random functions. For any text written without the
document key, condition on the selected carriers, probe pairs, responses, and target bits. The
orientation bits remain independent fair coins. Hence the $m_i$ are independent
$\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)$ variables, $T\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(|P|,\tfrac12)$, and
thresholding Eq.~\eqref{eq:test} at $\alpha$ gives false-positive rate at most $\alpha$ for every
document length and carrier count.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Why adaptive carrier selection is allowed.}
The text and model may determine the number of carriers, the probe pair, and the response size.
What matters is that each choice is fixed before the independent orientation bit is used. Flipping
$\epsilon_i$ then flips the match whenever $g_i(y_i)\neq0$; the tie rule supplies a fresh fair bit
otherwise. The detector therefore needs neither an asymptotic approximation nor a calibration
corpus. The guarantee still assumes text produced independently of the key and domain separation
among the key's several roles.
