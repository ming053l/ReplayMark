\section{ReTrace}
\label{sec:method}

One requirement organises everything in this section. The verifier holds only the
finished text, the model and the key --- so whatever quantity the embedder steers toward
during generation, the verifier must later be able to recompute \emph{bit for bit} from
the finished text. We first construct an observable with exactly that property
(\S\ref{sec:observable}), then a statistic over it whose null distribution is known
exactly rather than asymptotically (\S\ref{sec:theory}), then ask how generated text can
be made to score highly on it. The obvious embedding fails for measurable reasons
(\S\ref{sec:v1}), and what those measurements leave standing is the resampling embedder
of \S\ref{sec:embedding}.

\subsection{The observable}
\label{sec:observable}

Let $y$ be a generated span and $K$ a secret key. For a block $B$ of $y$, the key derives
$L$ \emph{ablation patterns} $D_1,\dots,D_L$ over the context preceding $B$, and a pair
$(u,v)$ among them. Writing $\mathcal{C}_\ell$ for the context with $B$, everything after
$B$, and $D_\ell$ masked, the \emph{challenge contrast} at position $i\in B$ is
\begin{equation}
g_i(w) \;=\; \log p_\theta\!\left(w \mid \mathcal{C}_v\right) - \log p_\theta\!\left(w \mid \mathcal{C}_u\right),
\qquad w \in \mathcal{V}.
\label{eq:g}
\end{equation}
In words: how much more does the model expect $w$ at position $i$ when the key has hidden
one part of the context than when it has hidden the other.

The shape of $\mathcal{C}_\ell$ is dictated by the reproducibility requirement.
Mid-generation, when the embedder consults Eq.~\eqref{eq:g}, every block after $B$ is
still \texttt{[MASK]}; if the verifier later recomputed the contrast on the finished
text without re-masking that tail, it would condition on tokens that did not exist when
the embedder acted, and the two sides would be reading different functions. So
$\mathcal{C}_\ell$ masks $B$ and everything after it, in every arm. Masking $B$ itself
buys a second property for free: the contrast becomes \emph{invariant} to whatever is
committed inside $B$, so one table, computed once, serves the block's entire decoding
and is reproduced identically from the finished text. The ablations, for the same
reason, lie only in the region that is already final --- $D_\ell \subset$ prompt
$\cup\, B_{<b}$, which the verifier has verbatim. Within that region the pair is chosen
for signal: one pattern ablates the context nearest the block, the other the farthest,
and because recency dominates masked-token prediction, the two arms disagree far more
than two random ablations would, widening the dynamic range of $g$.

\subsection{The statistic}
\label{sec:theory}

The contrast turns each position into a signed answer; the statistic aggregates the
answers so that chance is exactly quantifiable. Each position carries an independent
keyed \emph{orientation bit} $\epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}$ --- the hidden coin fixing which
sign of $g_i$ counts as agreement --- and a target $w_i\in\{\pm1\}$ carrying the message
bit ($w_i \equiv +1$ when only provenance is claimed). Aggregation is a plain count of
agreements,
\begin{equation}
m_i \;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[\,w_i\,\epsilon_i\,g_i(y_i) > 0\,\right],
\qquad
T \;=\; \sum_{i\in P} m_i ,
\label{eq:m}
\end{equation}
and a count rather than an average deliberately: the per-position contrast is
heavy-tailed, so an average is dominated by its largest values and sign steering has no
leverage on it, whereas a count gives every position exactly one unit.

Why is chance exactly quantifiable? Because for a text produced without the key, each
$m_i$ is a comparison against a coin that was never consulted:

\begin{proposition}[Exact null]
\label{prop:null}
Let the challenge patterns, the per-position pair selection, the carrier set, the group
assignment, the tie coins and the orientation bits each be derived from the key under
\emph{separate PRF domains}. Fix a text $y$ independent of the key, and condition on the
carrier set, the selected pairs, and the challenge magnitudes --- all of which are functions
of $y$ and the non-orientation domains only. Under the ideal-PRF model the orientation bits
are then independent Rademacher variables independent of everything conditioned on, so the
$m_i$ are i.i.d.\ $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)$ and
$T \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(|P|, \tfrac12)$ exactly.
\end{proposition}

The result needs no calibration corpus and no model-specific null estimate, but its
hypotheses have to be engineered, not assumed. The separate PRF domains let the
conditioning go through: pattern choice and pair selection are themselves keyed, and
sharing randomness with the orientation bits would leak the coin (pair selection uses
only the swap-invariant $S_i$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate}, so it is orientation-symmetric).
Exact ties $g_i(y_i)=0$ are resolved by a further keyed coin rather than scored as
misses, which would bias $T$ off the binomial. And presence is tested on its own pool
with $w_i \equiv +1$: summing agreement across payload groups cancels --- a balanced
message leaves a perfect watermark at the chance value $|P|/2$ --- so claimed-message
verification aligns each group to its bit and payload decoding thresholds each group
separately.

Rejection resampling is the embedder that satisfies this constraint. Generation follows
the reference schedule of the base model --- blocks in order, diffusion within a block,
positions committed in plain confidence order (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}) --- and
Algorithms~1 and~2 put the embedder beside the reference sampler it wraps, line for
line: the two differ only in the \textcolor{oursemph}{blue} lines --- no logit is
edited, no commitment is reordered, and deleting the blue lines recovers the base
model's decoder exactly. At a carrier position the committed token is chosen by
rejection sampling from the model's own conditional:
\begin{equation}
v^{(1)}, v^{(2)}, \ldots \sim p_\theta(\cdot \mid x), \qquad
\text{commit the first } v^{(r)} \text{ with } w_i\,\epsilon_i\,g_i(v^{(r)}) > 0,\quad r \le R,
\label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
and if all $R$ guided proposals are rejected, one further unconditional sample is committed as-is.
Acceptance may additionally require a \emph{plausibility floor} $p_i(v) \geq \kappa\,
p_i^{\max}$, which caps any carrier's cost at $-\log\kappa$ beyond the model's own argmax;
the floor is embedder-only and key-free --- the detector reads nothing but the committed
token --- so the null and the carrier rule are untouched. A retry costs no forward pass
--- the conditional row is unchanged --- so $R$ trades detection strength against
arithmetic alone, and the floor keeps that trade from spending the quality budget. Writing $A_i$ for the acceptance set and
$q_i$ for its mass, the per-position hit probability and committed-token distribution are
\begin{equation}
q_i = \Pr_{v \sim p_i}\!\left[\,w_i\,\epsilon_i\,g_i(v) > 0\,\right], \qquad
\Pr[m_i = 1] \;=\; 1 - (1 - q_i)^{R+1},
\label{eq:hit}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
v_i \;\sim\;
\bigl(1-(1-q_i)^{R}\bigr)\, p_i(\,\cdot \mid A_i)
\;+\; (1-q_i)^{R}\, p_i .
\label{eq:dist}
\end{equation} This \emph{does} condition
the sampling distribution on the acceptance predicate and is not claimed to be
distortion-free; what makes it cheap is that the support never leaves the model's own
conditional, and that the positions where the predicate binds hardest are exactly the
positions where it cannot be satisfied at all --- which fall through to the natural sample.

One question remains: which positions should the detector count? Not all of them ---
the acceptance mass $q_i$ is bimodal in practice (quartiles $0.02/0.60/0.98$), meaning
at most positions the model's plausible tokens all answer the challenge the same way.
Where that answer opposes the key, retries cannot help, and counting the position turns
a coin flip into a certain miss. Positions are therefore gated by the two-sided mass
\begin{equation}
S_i \;=\; 2\min\!\left(q_i^{+},\, q_i^{-}\right), \qquad
q_i^{\pm} = \Pr_{v \sim p_i^{\text{base}}}\!\left[\,\pm\, g_i(v) > 0\,\right],
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
and only carriers with $S_i > s_{\min}$ are counted. The gate respects both earlier
requirements at once: it is computed from the block-masked conditional, so the verifier
reconstructs the identical carrier set from the finished text (\S\ref{sec:observable});
and it is invariant under swapping the pair's arms and never sees the key's direction or
the message, so conditioning on it leaves the null of Proposition~\ref{prop:null} intact.
The challenge pair itself is chosen per position among the $\binom{L}{2}$ keyed patterns
by the same swap-invariant $S_i$, which raises the mean two-sided mass from $0.28$ (a
fixed near/far pair) to $0.45$.

The loop is now closed: every quantity in Algorithm~2's blue lines is a function of
text, model and key alone (\S\ref{sec:observable}), the count they feed carries an
exact null (\S\ref{sec:theory}), and the only freedom the embedder exercises ---
choosing among the model's own proposals --- is the one \S\ref{sec:v1} showed a
well-trained denoiser leaves open.
