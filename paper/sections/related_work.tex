\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Masked diffusion language models.}
Masked DLMs generate text by repeatedly predicting unresolved positions and committing a subset
of them. This extends discrete diffusion to text~\citep{ho2020ddpm,austin2021d3pm,
lou2024sedd,sahoo2024mdlm,shi2024simplified,ou2025radd} and now supports instruction-following
models including LLaDA and Dream~\citep{nie2025llada,zhu2025llada15,ye2025dream,
labs2025mercury}. Which positions commit, and in what order, affects both quality and speed
~\citep{arriola2025blockdiffusion,kim2025ordering,wu2026fastdllm,wei2026slowfast,
benhamu2025entropy,lee2026commitlocallyexitglobally}. We therefore treat the deployed schedule as
a constraint: ReplayMark observes the model's masked-token predictions but does not choose a new
commit order.

\paragraph{Watermarks encoded in tokens.}
Autoregressive watermarks usually bias a keyed vocabulary subset
~\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark} or couple each draw to keyed randomness
~\citep{aaronson2023gpt,fu2024gumbelsoft,kuditipudi2024distortionfree}. Their detectors are cheap
because a key--token calculation needs no model call, and later work characterises when those
detectors are reliable or undetectable~\citep{kirchenbauer2024reliability,zhao2024provable,
christ2024undetectable}. DLM methods adapt this idea by removing prefix dependence, averaging a
red--green bias over unresolved context, sampling with keyed randomness, or encoding information
in the denoising order~\citep{chen2025orderagnostic,gloaguen2026watermarking,
bagchi2025discrete,wu2025dmark,raban2026lrdwm,hong2026dgmark}. Our closest baseline, dgMARK,
changes which mask resolves next and later reads that order from the text. ReplayMark instead
keeps the order fixed and asks the checkpoint to reproduce its response.

\paragraph{Detectors that use a model.}
Model access can support richer tests than a key--token hash. Likelihood-ratio detectors compare
watermarked and base conditionals~\citep{hu2024unbiased}; access to the model can improve a
Neyman--Pearson score over a key-only Gumbel statistic~\citep{fernandez2023bricks}; GaussMark
verifies a keyed weight perturbation with a forward--backward pass~\citep{block2025gaussmark};
and model-theft watermarks query a suspect system~\citep{zhao2023invisible}. Outside watermarking,
DetectGPT scores text using log probabilities from the model of interest and perturbed versions of
the passage; replacing the source model with a surrogate weakens detection
~\citep{mitchell2023detectgpt}. These model-assisted detectors are most natural when a provider or
central verifier can afford model access. ReplayMark shares that operational setting but adds a
keyed generation channel and a document-level test. It leaves the checkpoint unchanged and keys
the contexts presented to it, so one deployed model can serve many keys.

\paragraph{Finite-sample detection and editing.}
Watermark tests increasingly replace asymptotic $z$-scores with exact tails, permutation tests,
or corrected maxima over windows~\citep{fernandez2023bricks,kuditipudi2024distortionfree,
kirchenbauer2024reliability}. After editing, a statistic that concentrates on surviving regions
can outperform a document-wide sum~\citep{li2025editrobust}. ReplayMark uses an exact binomial
tail for the unedited detector and compares document-wide, block-local, and windowed aggregation
under editing in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.
