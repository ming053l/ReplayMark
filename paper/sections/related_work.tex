\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Diffusion language models.}
Discrete and masked diffusion objectives carry diffusion~\citep{ho2020ddpm} over to text
\citep{austin2021d3pm,lou2024sedd,sahoo2024mdlm,shi2024simplified,ou2025radd} and now support
instruction-following systems such as LLaDA and Dream
\citep{nie2025llada,zhu2025llada15,ye2025dream,labs2025mercury}. Since commit order affects
quality and throughput~\citep{arriola2025blockdiffusion,kim2025ordering,wu2026fastdllm,
wei2026slowfast,benhamu2025entropy,lee2026commitlocallyexitglobally}, accelerators treat the
schedule as a tunable degree of freedom; Shibboleth treats it as an invariant and uses
masked-token reconstruction only to define a probe it can later repeat.

\paragraph{Watermarking autoregressive text.}
Autoregressive watermarks bias keyed token subsets~\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark} or couple
sampling to keyed randomness~\citep{aaronson2023gpt,fu2024gumbelsoft,
kuditipudi2024distortionfree}, with later work characterising their reliability and
undetectability~\citep{kirchenbauer2024reliability,zhao2024provable,christ2024undetectable}. Detectors of this family are inexpensive precisely because a
key--token hash needs no generator, yet that hash is defined over a resolved prefix and does not
transfer unchanged to a context in which most token positions are still masked.

\paragraph{Watermarking diffusion language models.}
DLM-specific methods restore a well-defined keyed partition by other means, whether by removing
prefix dependence~\citep{chen2025orderagnostic}, by averaging a red--green bias over unresolved
context~\citep{gloaguen2026watermarking}, by keyed sampling~\citep{bagchi2025discrete}, or by
exploiting the decoding order itself~\citep{wu2025dmark,raban2026lrdwm,hong2026dgmark}. We
compare against dgMARK, the strongest order-steering baseline with a zero-inference
detector. Shibboleth differs from all of them in where the evidence resides, since
dgMARK spends the DLM's freedom over commit order and reads the outcome off the text, whereas
Shibboleth spends nothing from the schedule and instead has the verifier rerun the exact checkpoint,
buying a statistic bound to model behaviour at the price of a detector that no longer runs on
text alone.

\paragraph{Detector statistics.}
Detector design has moved past the asymptotic $z$-test toward exact
tails~\citep{fernandez2023bricks}, permutation $p$-values for alignment
statistics~\citep{kuditipudi2024distortionfree}, and union-bound corrections for windowed
maxima~\citep{kirchenbauer2024reliability}; when edits leave evidence in only a fraction of
the text, sum-based pooling is provably dominated by statistics that concentrate on the
surviving fraction~\citep{li2025editrobust}. Shibboleth's binomial tail is an exact test, and
Section~\ref{sec:calibration} measures the sparse-signal alternatives against it.

\paragraph{Detection that consults the model.}
Every DLM watermark above detects from the text and key alone, but detectors that pay more for
their evidence are not new: edit-distance detection runs quadratic alignment under thousands
of permutation resamples~\citep{kuditipudi2024distortionfree}, model access makes the
Neyman--Pearson score strictly dominate the key-only Gumbel
statistic~\citep{fernandez2023bricks}, likelihood-ratio detection reads both the watermarked
and base conditionals~\citep{hu2024unbiased}, GaussMark keys a Gaussian weight perturbation
and verifies with one forward--backward pass~\citep{block2025gaussmark}, and model-theft
watermarks assume the verifier can query the suspect system~\citep{zhao2023invisible}. Unlike
GaussMark, which ships a perturbed checkpoint, Shibboleth leaves the released weights as they
are and puts the key in the questions asked of them, so one deployed model serves every key.
