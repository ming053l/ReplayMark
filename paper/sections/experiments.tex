\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:baselines}

Following dgMARK's evaluation organization, we first specify data, generation, metrics, and
baselines, then analyze detectability, quality, distributional effects, and robustness.

\subsection{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{Dataset, model, and environment.}
We use \texttt{GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Instruct} in fp16 on one TITAN RTX. C4
\texttt{realnewslike} prompts are truncated to 300 characters, matching dgMARK's protocol.
Unless stated otherwise, generation produces 512 tokens in 32-token blocks with one
denoising step per token. Principal ReTrace results use 20--40 prompts; the comparison
table reports every sample size rather than treating these modest runs as population-level
estimates.

\paragraph{Sampling and ReTrace configuration.}
The reference sampler uses full-vocabulary multinomial sampling at temperature $0.8$ and
the native confidence schedule. ReTrace adds eight ablation patterns, threshold
$s_{\min}=0.5$, retry budget $R$, and optional plausibility floor $\kappa$. Every document
uses an HMAC-derived key. We test provenance detection ($w_i=+1$), not payload recovery.

\paragraph{Metrics and baselines.}
Detectability is TPR at analytic FPR thresholds using Eq.~\eqref{eq:test}. Quality is
GPT-2-large perplexity paired with a control using the same schedule and temperature; we
report $\exp(\operatorname{mean}(\mathrm{NLL}_{\rm wm}-\mathrm{NLL}_{\rm ctrl}))$.
Pairs failing the decoded-length rule are excluded. Compute is the number of full sequence
evaluations, independent of batching. We locally reproduce KGW
\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark} under strictly left-to-right DLM decoding and dgMARK
\citep{hong2026dgmark} under confidence decoding.

\subsection{Experimental analyses}

\paragraph{Watermark detectability.}
At 512 tokens, $R=1$ reaches $0.43$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR. On 30 held-out prompts, increasing
$R$ from 2 to 4 to 8 raises TPR from $0.73$ to $0.83$ to $0.87$
(Figure~\ref{fig:operating}b). This agrees with Eq.~\eqref{eq:hit}: repeated draws increase
the chance of reaching the requested response set.

\paragraph{Effect of sampling strength.}
Retries require no extra DLM evaluations because they reuse the current row, but unfloored
PPL ratios rise from $1.14\times$ at $R=2$ to $1.40\times$ at $R=4$ and $1.61\times$ at
$R=8$. The plausibility floor trades some signal for quality: $(R,\kappa)=(8,0.1)$ obtains
$0.70$ TPR with a $0.87\times$ ratio, while $(16,0.05)$ obtains $0.80$ TPR with a
$0.74\times$ ratio. These below-one ratios mean only that GPT-2-large prefers the selected
high-probability tokens; they do not establish better semantics.

\paragraph{Effect of generation length.}
Longer outputs supply more carrier trials. At $R=1$, moving from 256 to 512 tokens raises
TPR at $1\%$ FPR from $0.25$ to $0.40$ in a separate run. At 1024 tokens,
$(R,\kappa)=(8,0.1)$ reaches $0.75$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR and $0.69$ at $0.1\%$, with a
$0.83\times$ PPL ratio. This setting is less conclusive: only 8 of 16 pairs pass the
decoded-length rule, and bigram repetition rises from $0.565$ in the control to $0.623$.
Evidence therefore scales with length, but generation stability becomes a confound.

\paragraph{Direct effect on the output distribution.}
Unlike dgMARK's order preference, RAR directly performs rejection sampling. Equation
\eqref{eq:dist} states the changed distribution, and the floor constrains only guided
acceptances. The unchecked fallback preserves termination but can emit any live proposal.
Thus \emph{model-proposed} does not mean distribution preserving.

\paragraph{Text generation quality.}
For $(R,\kappa)=(8,0.1)$, decoded bigram repetition is $0.33$ versus $0.36$ for its matched
control. This and PPL provide only surface-level evidence; no downstream-task or human
evaluation was run. We therefore report a measured PPL operating point, not an
imperceptibility claim.

\paragraph{Comparison and computational overhead.}
KGW reaches $0.93$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR and dgMARK reaches $0.74$ ($0.86$ with three-beam
lookahead), both above comparable ReTrace settings (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}). ReTrace
generation costs $656/512=1.28\times$ the reference sequence-evaluation budget and
verification costs 144 masked evaluations; KGW and dgMARK detection uses none. KGW,
however, requires left-to-right generation, and its repeated bigram events must be
deduplicated; without that correction its control measured $37\%$ FPR. The dgMARK
three-beam row costs roughly four generation evaluations per token. These are local
reproductions, not performance ceilings.

\paragraph{When is ReTrace preferable?}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} shows no universal win: token-based detectors are cheaper and
usually stronger. ReTrace instead improves two constrained operating points. First, it
keeps native blockwise decoding. The KGW-compatible left-to-right control itself incurs a
$1.17\times$ PPL cost relative to block decoding and exhibits high repetition, while
dgMARK obtains its signal by changing the position order. Second, ReTrace's model-driven
carrier selection remains compatible with an exact finite-sample null; no empirical
threshold calibration is needed. Relative to dgMARK three-beam, ReTrace also uses a lower
generation budget ($1.28\times$ versus roughly $4\times$ reference decoding), although its
$1\%$-FPR TPR is lower ($0.80$ versus $0.86$) and its verifier is far more expensive.
ReTrace is thus preferable only when schedule fidelity and model-bound exact verification
outweigh detector cost and edit robustness.

\paragraph{Detector checks.}
The exact-null result is analytical. In finite control arms, the 30-document retry run and
40-document $R=1$ run each yield zero detections at the $1\%$ threshold, too few samples to
estimate a $1\%$ FPR precisely. On the same cached $R=1$ outputs, gated sign count and two
likelihood-ratio variants each reach $0.38$ TPR; counting all positions falls to $0.33$.
We retain the simpler gated binomial detector.

\paragraph{Robustness to post-editing.}
On 20 $R=1$ documents, same-model argmax re-denoising of a random $10\%$ of positions
reduces TPR at $1\%$ FPR from $0.35$ to $0.05$; deleting $10\%$ has the same measured
effect. Early edits alter the fixed prefix used by later RRBs. ReTrace therefore does not
currently support claims of edit robustness.

\paragraph{Why not post-hoc substitution?}
Across 36 settings that replace tokens after ordinary generation, the median selected
position has one admissible token at likelihood budget $\tau=3$; the mean is $1.6$ at
$\tau=3$ and $5.6$ at $\tau=6$. The best setting within a $1.35\times$ PPL budget reaches
only $0.10$ TPR at $1\%$ FPR. This evaluated model's sharp conditionals leave little room
for forced replacement, motivating steering during sampling and an unchecked fallback.
